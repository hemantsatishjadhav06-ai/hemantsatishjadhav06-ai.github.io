Flats in Tellapur for Sale 2026: Prices, Projects & Buyer Guide

By Neopolis Infra Developers · 2026-08-26

Tellapur has become the corridor West Hyderabad buyers reach for when they want space and value without leaving the Gachibowli–Financial District orbit. A few kilometres further out than Narsingi or Kokapet, it trades a slightly longer commute for noticeably larger homes at noticeably lower prices — which is exactly why its big branded townships keep selling. If you are searching for flats in Tellapur for sale in 2026, this guide covers what is actually available, what prices really look like, how the landlord-share route lowers your cost, and the checks to run before you book.

Why Tellapur is West Hyderabad's value play

Tellapur's appeal is simple to state: it sits close enough to the western employment core to be a realistic daily commute, but far enough out that land — and therefore flats — cost less than in Kokapet, Narsingi or Nanakramguda. For a buyer comparing like-for-like, that gap usually means a bigger home, a higher floor, or a better-amenity project for the same budget.

The corridor has also matured past its "upcoming" phase. Large gated townships from established developers have delivered or are delivering here, social infrastructure has followed, and the Nallagandla neighbourhood next door gives Tellapur a base of schools, hospitals and retail that a genuinely new suburb would lack. For families and IT professionals priced out of the corridors nearer the office core, Tellapur is often the sensible landing spot.

What types of flats are available in Tellapur

Tellapur skews towards spacious, family-sized configurations — part of why buyers who want room choose it:

  • 2 BHK flats — commonly 950–1,480 sq ft, the entry point for first-time buyers and investors targeting IT-professional tenants.
  • 3 BHK flats — the core of supply, frequently 1,600–2,470 sq ft. Most large Tellapur communities are designed around the 3 BHK buyer, and the sizes here tend to be more generous than in the pricier corridors nearer the core.
  • 4 BHK and larger — premium townships offer 4 BHK units of 2,400 sq ft and up, often on higher floors with park or skyline views.

Both under-construction and ready-to-move options exist in depth. Under-construction generally offers keener pricing and staged payments; ready-to-move removes construction-period rent overlap and possession risk. The choice depends on your timeline and cash flow rather than any universal rule.

The landlord-share route: same flat, lower price

Most large Tellapur projects are joint developments — a landowner contributes the land, a developer builds, and the finished flats are split between them. The developer's share sells at card rate through the sales office. The landowner's share — often 30–45% of units — sells separately, and because the landowner's cost basis is the land rather than construction-plus-marketing, there is usually more room to negotiate on price.

A landlord-share flat in Tellapur is the same apartment, in the same tower, with the same amenities as the developer-sold unit beside it. Bought correctly, it typically costs 8–14% less than comparable resale — and because Tellapur is already the value corridor, that discount stacks on top of an entry price that is lower to begin with. The condition is diligence: you must confirm the specific unit belongs to the landowner's allocation and that the title is clean. Connectivity: what living in Tellapur actually feels like

Tellapur's connectivity is better than its "outer corridor" reputation suggests. The Financial District and Gachibowli sit roughly 5–6 km away — typically a 10–20 minute drive via Nallagandla Road and the Outer Ring Road under normal traffic. The ORR itself links Tellapur onward to Kollur, Patancheru and the airport corridor, so a trip to Rajiv Gandhi International Airport avoids city traffic entirely.

On the metro, the nearest operational station is Raidurg, about 4 km away and the current end of the line — close enough to be useful for onward travel into the city, though the last-mile still needs a vehicle. As with the rest of West Hyderabad, road widening and interchange upgrades continue to favour this belt. None of this guarantees future prices — no infrastructure story ever does — but it explains why Tellapur's commute case keeps improving rather than standing still.

Micro-localities: where in Tellapur to look

Tellapur is not one uniform market, and the pocket you choose shapes both price and lifestyle:

  • Nallagandla border — the most established edge, with schools, hospitals and retail already operating; strongest for immediate liveability.
  • Tellapur core / Osman Nagar side — the heart of the big-township supply, where most new gated communities are concentrated.
  • ORR-facing belt — units with the quickest access to the ORR service roads, favoured by daily commuters to the Financial District.
  • Interior pockets — slightly earlier in their development cycle, which often means sharper pricing for similar specifications.

If your priority is appreciation, proximity to the Nallagandla–Gachibowli axis has historically led the corridor. If your priority is immediate liveability, the pockets bordering Nallagandla's completed social infrastructure deserve a look first.

Amenities and lifestyle in Tellapur's gated communities

Tellapur competes hard on scale. Its signature is the large branded township — projects of many towers set around 40,000+ sq ft clubhouses, swimming pools, sports facilities, co-working lounges and landscaped central greens, with a dedicated park precinct in some layouts. For families, reputed schools and hospitals such as Citizens Specialty Hospital are within a short drive, and organised retail has followed the residential growth. When comparing two flats at similar prices, weigh the maintenance cost per sq ft against the amenity load — a bigger clubhouse is only a benefit if you will actually use it.

Rental demand and yield: the investor's view

If you are buying a flat in Tellapur partly or wholly as an investment, the tenant story is straightforward. Tellapur draws renters from the Financial District, Gachibowli and HITEC City employment base — typically dual-income technology households who want a newer, larger, amenity-rich home and are willing to trade a few extra minutes of commute for the space. That profile favours 2 and 3 BHK units in branded communities, which let quickly when priced sensibly.

Gross rental yields across West Hyderabad's apartment market are generally modest — residential yields in Indian metros usually sit in the low single digits — so the investment case rests primarily on entry price and appreciation. This is where Tellapur's value positioning plus the landlord-share route compound: a lower entry price to begin with, then 8–14% below comparable resale on top, improves both the effective yield on your capital and the margin of safety if the market cools. Treat any specific yield figure you are quoted as a scenario, not a promise, and run the numbers on the actual unit with current rents from comparable towers.

Tellapur vs Narsingi vs Kokapet: choosing between them

Buyers shortlisting Tellapur are usually weighing it against the corridors nearer the core, and each serves a different priority. Kokapet, anchored by the Neopolis high-rise layout, is the premium play — closest to the Financial District, strongest branding, highest per-square-foot pricing. Narsingi sits in the middle: real ORR connectivity and deep supply at a price below Kokapet. Tellapur is the value end — the most home for your money, with a commute that is longer but still realistic.

A practical way to decide: fix your commute tolerance first, then your budget, then compare like-for-like configurations across two corridors within those limits. Many buyers who start in Kokapet or Narsingi end up in Tellapur once they see how much more space the same budget buys a few minutes further along the ORR — and buyers who prize proximity over size make the opposite move. There is no wrong answer, only the trade-off that fits your life.

Step-by-step: how to buy a flat in Tellapur safely

  1. Fix your budget and financing first. Get a home-loan pre-approval so you can negotiate as a ready buyer. Banks lend on landlord-share units once allocation documents are verified.
  2. Shortlist 3–5 units across at least two projects. Compare carpet area (not just super built-up), floor, facing, and total cost including parking, amenities, and GST where applicable.
  3. Verify the paperwork before any advance. For any Tellapur flat: RERA registration, sanctioned plans (HMDA/GHMC), clear title on the parent land, and the occupancy certificate for ready buildings. For landlord-share units, add the registered Joint Development Agreement and the unit-allocation schedule naming your exact flat in the landowner's share, plus written confirmation the unit is unencumbered.
  4. Agreement of sale, then registration. The standard sub-registrar process applies. NRIs can complete most steps remotely through a registered Power of Attorney.
  5. Take possession formally. Snag the unit, confirm amenity handover status, and collect the complete document set.

Skipping step three is the most common and most expensive mistake buyers make. Never let a discount rush you past verification — a seller who pushes you to register faster than your lawyer can read the title is telling you something.

Myths vs facts about buying in Tellapur

"Tellapur is too far to commute." At roughly 5–6 km and 10–20 minutes to the Financial District and Gachibowli under normal traffic, Tellapur is a realistic daily commute — closer than many buyers assume before they drive it.

"Landlord-share flats are riskier." The concept is not the risk; unverified paperwork is. A landlord-share unit with a verified JDA allocation and clean title is legally identical in ownership terms after registration.

"Cheaper corridor means weaker appreciation." Value corridors with improving connectivity have historically been where the percentage gains come from, precisely because they start from a lower base. Entry price and holding period decide your outcome.

"You must buy through the project sales office." The developer's office sells the developer's share. The landowner's share sells separately — often at better prices — through desks that hold that inventory.

Documentation checklist before you register

Keep this list beside you through the purchase. For any flat in Tellapur: the title-deed chain and an independent legal opinion on the parent land; the RERA registration certificate and the project's committed possession date; HMDA or GHMC sanctioned plans matching what is actually built; the occupancy certificate for ready buildings; and receipts confirming property tax and utility dues are clear. For landlord-share units, add the registered Joint Development Agreement and any supplementary agreements; the unit-allocation schedule naming your exact flat in the landowner's share; confirmation the unit is free of mortgages or liens; and the seller's identity and authorisation documents. If any single document is missing or "coming soon," pause until it exists. A genuine seller will never resist verification.
